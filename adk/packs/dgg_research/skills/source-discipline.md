# Grading a source

Sources are graded, not vibed. The grade is about the source's RELATIONSHIP to
the event, never about whether you agree with it.

**Primary** — the artifact itself. A filing, an order, a docket entry, an
executive action, a recording, the actual post. If you are holding the thing
that happened, you are holding primary.

**Reporting** — somebody describing a primary they hold. Useful, and it is not
the artifact. Resolve to the artifact before you record the claim: the largest
documented failure mode in this project is holding a document and never reading
what it cites.

**Commentary** — somebody reasoning about reporting. It can tell you where to
look. It is never the evidence.

## Stated intent is not fact

What someone SAID they would do and what is DOCUMENTED to have happened are
different kinds of claim, and they never merge. Not in a column, not in a
summary, not in a sentence that quietly does both. If a source lets you infer
one from the other, that inference is your analysis and it is labelled as such.

For a stated-intent record the WORDS are the evidence, so the quote has to be
exact and it has to be present in the capture you cite.

## Quote first, interpret after

Locate and slice; do not retype. Return the anchors and let the tool cut the
text — transcription is the one part of this job a script does perfectly. A
mistyped anchor fails the lookup, which is what you want; a mistyped quote
succeeds and is wrong.

The quote goes in verbatim, in quotation marks, with its source. Your reading
of it goes after, in your own voice, where a reader can disagree with the
reading without doubting the quote.

## Findings against the thesis stay in

Near the front. A record that only contains what supports its own conclusion is
not a record, and the hostile reader you are writing for will find the omission
faster than they will find the argument.
